ExRL: Sample-Efficient Online Adaptation of Long-Chunk Policies via Execution Length Control

ExRL adapts long-chunk robot policies by learning a lightweight execution-length adapter that decides how many actions from each predicted chunk to execute, replacing high-dimensional action optimization with a low-dimensional discrete decision. It improves diverse long-chunk policies, including π0.5, on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks with limited physical interaction.

Xu Luo, Jiaying Yang, Hao Wu, Junlin Xie, Zijin Hong, Ji Zhang, Lianli Gao, Jingkuan Song, arXiv, 2025

TL;DR

This paper addresses the issue of reduced sample efficiency when online-adapting robot policies that predict long action chunks. Instead of modifying the actions themselves, it introduces a lightweight RL adapter (ExRL) that determines only the execution length (kkk) of the predicted chunk to be executed. Compared to existing methods, it achieves over 80–90% success rates on real-world tasks within tens of minutes and is applicable regardless of the base policy’s architecture.

Problem Definition

In recent robot learning, models such as π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​, Diffusion Policy (DP), and ACT predict action chunks consisting of dozens of steps in a single inference. While this improves computational efficiency, performance degrades significantly when executing the entire long chunk in real-world deployment due to environmental uncertainty or initial errors.

Existing RL-based adaptation methods must optimize residuals or latent variables in the high-dimensional primitive action space. As the chunk length HHH increases, the dimensionality of the optimization problem grows, leading to extremely low online sample efficiency. In particular, for VLA (Vision-Language-Action) models like π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​, which have high inference costs, iterative candidate generation and evaluation for high-dimensional action optimization are impractical.

To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes a new framework that can adapt policies in a sample-efficient manner without altering the structure or output distribution of the base policy.

Core Idea

The intuition behind ExRL (Execution-Length RL) is to learn “when to stop.” From the HHH-step chunk at:t+Ha_{t:t+H}at:t+H​ predicted by the base policy, it selects and executes the length k∈{1,…,H}k \in \{1, \dots, H\}k∈{1,…,H} that best fits the current state sts_tst​.

The key advantage of this approach is that the dimensionality of the optimization space remains low (scalar or discrete category). As the chunk length HHH increases, expressivity grows, but the complexity of the action space that the RL agent must learn does not change. From the perspective of an SMDP (Semi-Markov Decision Process), this is similar to inducing a high-level decision-making problem without modifying the primitive MDP.

Methodology

Execution Length Policy (πex\pi_{\text{ex}}πex​)

ExRL learns a lightweight adapter that takes the state sts_tst​ and the predicted action chunk at:t+Ha_{t:t+H}at:t+H​ as inputs and outputs a distribution over the execution length kkk. This adapter operates independently of the base policy, which can be kept in a frozen state.

Duration-aware Q-learning

The critic network Qϕ(st,at:t+H,k)Q_\phi(s_t, a_{t:t+H}, k)Qϕ​(st​,at:t+H​,k) estimates the expected reward when executing length kkk from a specific state and chunk. In the TD target calculation, the discount factor γkt\gamma^{k_t}γkt​ is applied according to the selected length ktk_tkt​, accurately reflecting the impact of long executions on future value.

Implementation Variants: ExRL-Q and ExRL-SAC

  1. ExRL-Q (Value-based): Selects the length with the maximum Q-value from the critic without an explicit actor network. kt=arg⁡max⁡k∈KQϕ(st,at:t+H,k)k_t = \arg\max_{k \in \mathcal{K}} Q_\phi(s_t, a_{t:t+H}, k)kt​=argmaxk∈K​Qϕ​(st​,at:t+H​,k) It leverages off-policy characteristics to maximize sample efficiency and adds a CQL (Conservative Q-Learning) regularization term to prevent overestimation of Q-values for lengths with insufficient support in the replay buffer.
  2. ExRL-SAC (Actor-Critic): Applies the discrete SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) framework. It learns an actor that outputs a categorical distribution πψ(k∣s,a)\pi_\psi(k|s, a)πψ​(k∣s,a) and a soft value function including an entropy bonus to promote exploration and prevent premature convergence.

Online BC (Optional)

Successful online rollout data can be used to update the base policy πbase\pi_{\text{base}}πbase​ itself via behavior cloning (BC). By reconstructing the primitive actions from successful trajectories into HHH-step chunk targets for training, this improves the fundamental performance of the base policy alongside execution length control.

Utilizing Offline Data

Offline trajectories, such as human demonstrations, are interpreted as virtual action chunks and added to the replay buffer. For each HHH-step interval, all possible prefix lengths k≤Hk \le Hk≤H are converted into experiences, providing initial experience for various execution lengths.

Ablation Studies

  • TD Target: Reward/discount calculations considering the selected length kkk are more learning-efficient than single-step processing.
  • Chunk Conditioning: Success rates are low with observation states alone, but adding predicted chunk information to the critic input significantly improves success rates.
  • Network Size: Similar learning curves are observed across widths of 128–2048, indicating a low burden for hyperparameter tuning.

Limitations

The limitations explicitly stated by the authors in the paper are as follows:

  • Real-world experiments were conducted with the base policy (π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​) frozen. That is, only ExRL was learned, and the base policy parameters were not updated. (A variant updating the base policy via Online BC was proposed, but the main real-world results are based on the frozen state.)
  • If the replanning frequency increases, the inference cost of the base policy may increase, potentially causing rollout delays. To mitigate this, physical time (including inference) was reflected in rewards and discounts for Stacking/Folding tasks.

Critical Review

Strengths

  • The direction of the lightweight execution length adapter is clear — It reduces the problem by selecting only the execution length kkk from the chunk predicted by a frozen base policy, rather than directly optimizing actions in a high-dimensional space. This design is explicitly stated to be policy-agnostic (Evidence: Sec 1, Sec 2).
  • Broad range of base policies, benchmarks, and baselines — Uses not only DP/ACT but also π0\pi_0π0​, π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​, π0\pi_0π0​-FAST, and Cosmos Policy, comparing against Random-k, fixed-k, AAC, Online BC, Policy Decorator, DSRL, PA-RL, OGPO, ResFiT, DICE-RL, etc., on Robomimic/Adroit/LIBERO (Evidence: Sec 3, Appendix F).
  • Interpretation experiments supporting the core mechanism — Demonstrates the value of length selection matching state-chunk via shuffle experiments and quantitatively analyzes feature aliasing and repetitive replanning issues (Evidence: Sec 5, Appendix D, Table VIII).
  • Relatively detailed setup information in the appendix — Provides algorithm pseudocode, task/demo scale, architecture, hyperparameters, and real-world training/evaluation settings, serving as a basis for reproducibility assessment (Evidence: Algorithm 1&2, Appendix A/B/G, Tables III-V/VII).

Weaknesses & Questions

  • Limited real-world generalization — Real-world results are limited to the frozen π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​ state and four tasks. It is not provided in the notes whether it was extended to other base policies, robot platforms, or more tasks (Evidence: Sec 4, Appendix G, Sec 6).
  • Lack of statistical uncertainty in simulation comparisons — The number of repeated runs, variance, and significance reports are not explicitly stated for major performance curves like Fig. 6/7. While analyses providing CIs exist (like Table VIII), it is difficult to confirm if the same level of uncertainty was reported for major baseline comparisons (Evidence: Sec 3, Appendix D) (Interpretation).
  • Effects of CQL and ExRL-Q/SAC selection are not separated — Although a CQL regularization term is introduced, no ablation separating its contribution is provided in the notes. Also, systematic comparison results showing the relative advantages of ExRL-Q and SAC variants are lacking (Evidence: Sec 2, Sec 5) (Interpretation).
  • Difficulty verifying fairness of baseline reimplementation — Some baselines like DSRL, PA-RL, OGPO, DICE-RL were implemented by the authors in PyTorch or connected to official learners, with settings differing by method. It is difficult to judge from the provided notes alone if comparisons were made under identical learning budgets and tuning levels (Evidence: Appendix F) (Interpretation).
  • Online BC (behavior cloning) may be confounded with core contributions — While the superiority of the ExRL-Q + Online BC combination is reported, if the effect of base policy updates and the execution length adapter effect are not completely separated, the claim of a “lightweight adapter” may be weakened (Evidence: Sec 2, Sec 3, Appendix E) (Interpretation).
  • Unclear information on public assets required for reproduction — The provided notes do not specify code repositories, dataset/demo public links, or total computational costs. Although Appendix G mentions reproducibility items, the actual status of public availability is unconfirmed (Evidence: Appendix G) (Interpretation).

Reproducibility

  • While Algorithm 1/2, hyperparameters (Table III-V/VII), and real-world settings (Appendix G) are provided in the notes, code/data public links and total computational costs are not specified.
