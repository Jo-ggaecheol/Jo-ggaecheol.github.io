Magic-W0: A Structured World–Action Foundation Model for Physical Intelligence

Magic-W0 is a world–action foundation model that couples structured physical state evolution—current 3D geometry, 3D motion, and future semantics—with continuous action generation through bidirectional layer-aligned interaction. It is pre-trained on diverse manipulation data and fine-tuned with limited downstream data for strong simulation and real-robot performance.

TL;DR

  • Magic-W0 addresses the limitations of World-Action Models (WAMs) that rely on future observation reconstruction or general latent prediction by structuring them into a Structured World Transition defined as Current State–Transition–Future State.
  • It connects VLM context, 3D geometry/motion, and Future Semantics via layer-aligned bidirectional attention to jointly learn continuous action generation and world transition.
  • It reports an average Score of 36.75 on RoboDojo-Sim (ranking first among compared methods) and an average success rate of 94.6% on real-robot fine-tuning (+2.8%p compared to π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​).

Problem Definition

Existing robotic manipulation models can be broadly categorized into three types. First, Action-centric Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily generate actions from current observations and language instructions, relying implicitly on world understanding. Second, Pixel-based WAMs generate actions while predicting future pixel observations but do not explicitly separate physical structures. Third, Latent WAMs predict the future using general latent representations, but the 3D geometry, motion, and semantics required for control are not sufficiently structured.

The problem Magic-W0 aims to solve is to move beyond these limitations by explicitly representing physical world state transitions as a Structured World Transition, ensuring that this representation interacts bidirectionally with continuous action generation. Additionally, it must integrate the state-action space to enable learning from heterogeneous embodiment data within a single model, such as first-person human manipulation, UMI (Universal Manipulation Interface), real robots, and simulations.

Core Idea

The core idea is to reframe robotic manipulation as the problem of “how does the world transition given an action in the current physical state?” Magic-W0 bundles the Current State, Transition, and Future State into a single structure. This structure serves as the condition for action generation while simultaneously allowing predicted world representations to provide feedback for action updates.

Wt,Δ=(Ct,Zt⏟Current State,Zt→t+Δ⏟Transition,Zt+Δ⏟Future State)\mathcal{W}_{t,\Delta} = \left( \underbrace{\mathbf{C}_t,\mathbf{Z}_t}_{\text{Current State}}, \underbrace{\mathbf{Z}_{t \rightarrow t+\Delta}}_{\text{Transition}}, \underbrace{\mathbf{Z}_{t+\Delta}}_{\text{Future State}} \right)Wt,Δ​=​Current StateCt​,Zt​​​,TransitionZt→t+Δ​​​,Future StateZt+Δ​​​​

Here, the Current State consists of VLM context and Current 3D Geometry, the Transition is represented by 3D Motion, and the Future State is represented by Future Semantics.

Methodology

Structured World Representation

We concretize each component of Wt,Δ\mathcal{W}_{t,\Delta}Wt,Δ​ defined above. Ct\mathbf{C}_tCt​ is the VLM context, including visual, linguistic, and proprioceptive states. Zt\mathbf{Z}_tZt​ represents the physical structure of the current scene as Current 3D Geometry, while Zt→t+Δ\mathbf{Z}_{t \rightarrow t+\Delta}Zt→t+Δ​ represents state transitions caused by actions as 3D Motion. Zt+Δ\mathbf{Z}_{t+\Delta}Zt+Δ​ represents the semantics of the future scene as Future Semantics.

These representations are defined in the feature space of frozen teacher models. Track4World provides geometry and motion features, while DINOv3 provides patch features for future frames. Latent targets are extracted from the teacher models only during training; during inference, internal streams generate them directly. Target features are unified to 256 tokens per view with a dimensionality of 1024.

Unified State-Action Interface

For cross-embodiment learning, joint angles for both arms, gripper openness, and end-effector positions and rotations are integrated into a single 34-dimensional vector.

x=[qL,gL,qR,gR,pLCt,rLCt,pRCt,rRCt]∈R34\mathbf{x} = [ \mathbf{q}_L, g_L, \mathbf{q}_R, g_R, \mathbf{p}_L^{C_t}, \mathbf{r}_L^{C_t}, \mathbf{p}_R^{C_t}, \mathbf{r}_R^{C_t} ] \in \mathbb{R}^{34}x=[qL​,gL​,qR​,gR​,pLCt​​,rLCt​​,pRCt​​,rRCt​​]∈R34

q\mathbf{q}q denotes 7-dimensional joint angles, ggg denotes 1-dimensional gripper openness, p\mathbf{p}p denotes the 3D end-effector position relative to the camera coordinate frame CtC_tCt​, and r\mathbf{r}r denotes 6D rotation encoding. Unobserved dimensions are padded with zeros and handled via masking. First-person human data lacks a robotic embodiment, so all 14 joint slots are invalid. UMI, real-robot, and simulation trajectories share 6 degrees of freedom (DoF) for both arms, with only the 7th joint slot for each arm remaining empty.

Action-World Temporal Alignment

Since action sampling rates ρi\rho_iρi​ vary across data sources, action chunks of the same length HHH may cover different intervals in the raw observation time series. Magic-W0 introduces source-aware action-world temporal alignment. In this stage, HHH is fixed at 50, and the future frame offset is determined as follows:

Δi=⌈Hρi⌉\Delta_i = \left\lceil \frac{H}{\rho_i} \right\rceilΔi​=⌈ρi​H​⌉

Samples are constructed based on an anchor time ttt. Multi-view observations o≤t\mathbf{o}_{\le t}o≤t​, language instructions lll, proprioceptive state st\mathbf{s}_tst​, action chunk at:t+H−1\mathbf{a}_{t:t+H-1}at:t+H−1​, and temporally corresponding future observations ot+Δi\mathbf{o}_{t+\Delta_i}ot+Δi​​ are used together. Current 3D Geometry supervision is extracted from the head camera view at time ttt, while Future Semantics supervision is extracted from available views at time t+Δit+\Delta_it+Δi​. 3D Motion supervision is extracted from the frame pair (ttt and t−Δit-\Delta_it−Δi​) specified in the paper; this notation appears to be for aligning action chunk coverage with future observations (interpretation).

Continuous actions are converted into chunk-level delta representations. Joints and end-effector positions use relative changes compared to the initial state, rotations use relative rotations, and grippers maintain absolute openness.

World-Action Co-Modeling Architecture

The model consists of a VLM backbone (Qwen3.5-2B), Semantic Stream, 3D Stream, and Action Expert. The total parameter count is 3.4B, with an overall depth of 24 layers. Each block comprises three Gated DeltaNet layers and one Attention layer, and this structure is repeated six times. Joint Attention is performed at layers 4, 8, …, 24.

In Joint Attention, the Key-Value pairs from the VLM, Semantic Stream, 3D Stream, and Action Expert are integrated.

(Kjointℓ,Vjointℓ)=Concat[(K,V)vlmℓ,(K,V)semℓ,(K,V)3dℓ,(K,V)actℓ](\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\ell},\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\ell}) = \mathrm{Concat}\big[ (\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})_{\mathrm{vlm}}^{\ell}, (\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})_{\mathrm{sem}}^{\ell}, (\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})_{3\mathrm{d}}^{\ell}, (\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})_{\mathrm{act}}^{\ell} \big](Kjointℓ​,Vjointℓ​)=Concat[(K,V)vlmℓ​,(K,V)semℓ​,(K,V)3dℓ​,(K,V)actℓ​]

The Semantic, 3D, and Action streams attend to the integrated Key-Value pairs using their own Queries.

Zmℓ+1=Fmℓ(Qmℓ,Kjointℓ,Vjointℓ),m∈{sem,3d,act}\mathbf{Z}_{m}^{\ell +1} = \mathcal{F}_{m}^{\ell} \big( \mathbf{Q}_{m}^{\ell}, \mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\ell}, \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\ell} \big), \quad m \in \{\mathrm{sem}, 3\mathrm{d}, \mathrm{act}\}Zmℓ+1​=Fmℓ​(Qmℓ​,Kjointℓ​,Vjointℓ​),m∈{sem,3d,act}

The visibility structure is asymmetric. The VLM does not attend to Expert streams, while Semantic/3D streams can access Action tokens. The Action Expert accesses World streams. During training, either the 3D or Semantic access of the Action Query is randomly masked to expose conditions similar to source masking during inference.

Training Objectives and Pre-training

Action generation uses Flow Matching. Normalized action chunks a\mathbf{a}a are represented as HHH tokens, and noisy actions are constructed as follows:

xτ=(1−τ)a+τϵ,τ∈[0,1]\mathbf{x}_\tau = (1-\tau)\mathbf{a} + \tau \epsilon, \quad \tau \in [0,1]xτ​=(1−τ)a+τϵ,τ∈[0,1]

The model outputs a velocity field vθ(xτ,τ)\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_\tau,\tau)vθ​(xτ​,τ), and the training objective is:

Lflow=Ea,ϵ,τ[∥vθ(xτ,τ)−u⋆∥Mact2],u⋆=ϵ−a\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{a}, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \tau} \left[ \left\| \mathbf{v}_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_\tau, \tau) - \mathbf{u}^{\star} \right\|_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{act}}}^{2} \right], \quad \mathbf{u}^{\star} = \epsilon - \mathbf{a}Lflow​=Ea,ϵ,τ​[∥vθ​(xτ​,τ)−u⋆∥Mact​2​],u⋆=ϵ−a

The World Loss differs by head. Future Semantics uses cosine distance, while Current 3D Geometry and 3D Motion use MSE after channel normalization (Layer Normalization).

ℓsem=1−cos⁡(z^,y)\ell_{\mathrm{sem}} = 1 - \cos(\hat{\mathbf{z}}, \mathbf{y})ℓsem​=1−cos(z^,y) ℓr=1d∥LN⁡(z^)−LN⁡(y)∥22,d=1024\ell_{r} = \frac{1}{d} \left\| \operatorname{LN}(\hat{\mathbf{z}}) - \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{y}) \right\|_2^2, \quad d=1024ℓr​=d1​∥LN(z^)−LN(y)∥22​,d=1024

The total loss combines Flow Matching, VQA, FAST discrete action supervision, and World Loss.

L=λflowLflow+λVQALVQA+λFASTLFAST+λsemLsem+λ3d(λgeoLgeo+λmotLmot)\mathcal{L} = \lambda_{\text{flow}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} + \lambda_{\text{VQA}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{VQA}} + \lambda_{\text{FAST}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{FAST}} + \lambda_{\text{sem}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{sem}} + \lambda_{\text{3d}} ( \lambda_{\text{geo}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}} + \lambda_{\text{mot}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{mot}} )L=λflow​Lflow​+λVQA​LVQA​+λFAST​LFAST​+λsem​Lsem​+λ3d​(λgeo​Lgeo​+λmot​Lmot​)

LFAST\mathcal{L}_{\text{FAST}}LFAST​ provides explicit robot action supervision to the VLM backbone, while LVQA\mathcal{L}_{\text{VQA}}LVQA​ is a standard autoregressive language modeling objective. Pre-training data mixes approximately 2.01 million manipulation episodes and approximately 2.61 million Vision-Language samples in a 9:1 ratio after filtering.

Gradient isolation is used during pre-training. The 3D Stream, Semantic Stream, and Action Expert can access the VLM context via joint attention, but continuous action and World representation losses do not backpropagate through the VLM context or its Key-Value projections. Consequently, VLM backbone updates are performed only for Vision-Language autoregressive objectives and FAST discrete action supervision. The visual encoder is trainable. Auxiliary objective weights start high and decay gradually.

Post-training and Inference

Post-training is performed via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the target domain. Initialized from pre-trained checkpoints, joint fine-tuning is conducted using all demo data from the target domain. In this stage, only continuous action and World representation losses are used; FAST discrete action supervision and Vision-Language corpus mixing are excluded. Gradient isolation is removed, allowing the VLM backbone, 3D/Semantic streams, Action Expert, and visual encoder to be updated jointly.

During inference, current multi-view observations, language instructions, and proprioceptive states are encoded into the VLM context, and Key-Value pairs for each layer are cached. An action chunk of length HHH is initialized from Gaussian noise, and continuous actions are generated by numerically integrating the Flow Matching velocity field. The default setting uses 10 uniform Euler steps from τ=1→0\tau=1 \to 0τ=1→0. At each integration step, the 3D/Semantic streams and Action Expert are recomputed based on the current action state. Evolving action hypotheses influence future 3D motion/semantic representations, and updated hidden states feed back into action velocity field predictions through layer-aligned interactions. Since the VLM does not attend to Expert streams, the context remains invariant during flow integration and is computed/cached only once at the start of decision-making. Teacher models are not loaded during inference; internal streams directly generate World representations.

RoboDojo-Sim

RoboDojo-Sim evaluates five dimensions—Generalization, Precision, Long-Horizon, Memory, and Open—using 42 simulation tasks. Magic-W0 ranks first among compared methods in both average Score and average SR. It particularly shows strengths in Generalization and Memory. This is interpreted as leveraging action-conditional World state changes for decision-making, going beyond local visual patterns of current observations.

LIBERO

On LIBERO, fine-tuning from pre-trained weights is used to compare with representative methods. Magic-W0 achieves an average SR of 99.1%, showing the highest SR in Spatial and Goal tasks. It is reported that joint modeling of 3D geometry and Vision-Language context is effective for providing spatial relationship information, and that the combination of 3D Motion and Future Semantics contributes to connecting manipulation actions, environmental changes, and goal states.

Real-robot

Real-robot experiments use five tasks: clothing folding, bottle standing, pen storage, object storage, and kitchen storage. Magic-W0 and π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​ use identical demos, learning budgets, observations, action spaces, and initial evaluation states, performing 100 independent trials per task.

Magic-W0’s average SR is 94.6%, which is 2.8%p higher than π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​‘s 91.8%. Per-task results show a tie at 100% for bottle standing, improvements from 90% to 95% for object storage, and from 91% to 95% for kitchen storage. Pen storage improves by +3%p, and clothing folding by +2%p. Advantages are notable in tasks requiring deformable object manipulation, multiple objects, and continuous state transitions.

World-Action Interaction Analysis

To verify whether layer-aligned cross-stream interaction is actually utilized, action replacement and connection masking experiments are conducted. Actions are replaced with those from other samples within the same batch, and a metric normalized by the average feature distance between samples in the batch is used to measure head output changes.

In action sensitivity analysis, Future Semantics reacts most strongly. In the low-noise region τ∈[0,0.2)\tau \in [0, 0.2)τ∈[0,0.2), normalized output changes for Future Semantics, 3D Motion, and Current 3D Geometry are 0.50, 0.37, and 0.26, respectively. In the high-noise region τ∈[0.8,1.0)\tau \in [0.8, 1.0)τ∈[0.8,1.0), they decrease to 0.14, 0.11, and 0.07, respectively. The Action Expert velocity field output reacts strongly in the range of 0.93–0.99 across all regions. Future Semantics loss increases by 73.5% upon action replacement in the lowest noise region and by 8.0% in the highest noise region. Without action replacement, Lsem\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}Lsem​ increases from 0.0741 to 0.0828.

Cross-stream dependency analysis shows that Future Semantics strongly depends on the 3D stream. Masking the 3D→Sem3D \to Sem3D→Sem connection increases Lsem\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}Lsem​ by +60.8%, whereas masking only the Sem→3DSem \to 3DSem→3D connection increases it by +2.6%. Blocking the Action path also increases loss, particularly highlighting the importance of the path where Action transmits information to Future Semantics via the 3D stream. Removing all cross-stream connections increases Lsem\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}Lsem​ by +95.3%.

Limitations

  • Dexterity for precise manipulation, i.e., handling high-dimensional action spaces and fine contact, remains a future task.
  • Integration of tactile/force signals has not yet been resolved.
  • Computational costs of multi-layer interaction and inference speed gaps must be addressed.

Critical Review

Strengths

  • Quantitative Mechanism Verification of Internal Interaction — Goes beyond simple performance comparison by quantitatively separating the contribution of layer-aligned cross-stream paths using Action Sensitivity (DmD_mDm​ metric) and Cross-Stream Dependence (Lsem\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sem}}Lsem​ change) experiments. Specifically, the asymmetry of +60.8% loss increase when masking 3d→sem3d \to sem3d→sem vs. +2.6% for sem→3dsem \to 3dsem→3d concretely demonstrates the information flow in structured representations (Evidence: Sec 6.3, Figures 10, 11).
  • Systematic Temporal Alignment for Heterogeneous Data Integration — Corrects differences in action sampling rates ρi\rho_iρi​ per source using Δi=⌈H/ρi⌉\Delta_i = \lceil H/\rho_i \rceilΔi​=⌈H/ρi​⌉, ensuring consistent construction of continuous actions and future world supervision from the same current state. Explicitly handles transitions between embodiments via the 34-dimensional interface and slot masking (Figure 7) (Evidence: Sec 4.2, Figure 7, Appendix A).
  • Consistent Performance Between Simulation and Real-Robot — RoboDojo-Sim average Score of 36.75 (Rank 1) and Real-Robot average SR of 94.6% (+2.8%p vs π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​) are derived from the same architecture, supporting the downstream transferability of structured world representations (Evidence: Table 1, Figure 9).

Weaknesses & Questions

  • Singularity of Real-Robot Baseline — Real-Robot experiments compare only with π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​, excluding other WAM/VLAs like Awomo-0.5 (WAM) or Simate-beta (VLA) that appeared in RoboDojo-Sim. It is impossible to determine whether the advantage of structured world representations is specific to this baseline or generalizable (Evidence: Sec 6 Real-Robot Experiments).
  • Lack of Statistical Significance and Variance Reporting — Although it states 100 independent trials per task (not explicitly stated in Tables/Figures), the number of repetitions, standard deviation, and confidence intervals are not reported anywhere in Table 1, Table 2, or Figure 9. It is impossible to verify if the +2.8%p difference is statistically significant (Evidence: Sec 6, Tables 1, 2, Figure 9).
  • LIBERO Benchmark Saturation — With an average SR of 99.1% (Spatial 99.4%, Goal 99.6%), it is nearly saturated. It is difficult to measure the differential contribution of structured world representations with these numbers, and there is a lack of harder benchmarks or failure case analysis (Evidence: Table 2).
  • Absence of Ablation for Entire Structured Representation — Cross-Stream Dependence analyzes dependencies of internal cross-stream paths, not a performance ablation comparing “Entire Structured World Transition vs. unstructured alternatives (pixel-based or simple latent).” The quantitative advantage of Contribution 1 is not independently isolated (Evidence: Sec 6.3, Figure 11; Figure 2 provides only conceptual comparison).
  • Inconsistency in 3D Motion Supervision Definition — The text specifies 3D Motion as the ”ttt and t−Δit-\Delta_it−Δi​ frame pair,” but it is ambiguous how comparison with past frames (t−Δit-\Delta_it−Δi​) represents action-induced transitions in a structure where the action chunk covers ttt to t+H−1t+H-1t+H−1. Given that future frame comparison is common in context, there is a gap between the definition in the original text and intuition (Evidence: Sec 4.2).
  • Lack of Quantification of Inference Cost — While the conclusion lists “resolving computational costs of multi-layer interaction and inference speed gaps” as a future task, actual latency, throughput, and training GPU hours for the current model are not reported anywhere in the paper. The discussion on VLM KV caching efficiency in Sec 5.2 is qualitative (Evidence: Sec 7 Conclusion, Sec 5.2).

Reproducibility

Code availability is not specified in the paper. Dataset composition (Tables 3, 4), hyperparameters (Appendix E: Muon/AdamW, learning rate 2×10−42\times10^{-4}2×10−4, loss weight scheduling Eq. 18), and teacher models (Track4World, DINOv3 ViT-L/16) are described in detail, but the absence of training computational costs (GPU hours) and absolute inference latency figures limits complete reproduction and efficiency comparison (Evidence: Appendices A, E; Sec 5.2).

Pushing Dexterity and Safety for In-Hand Object Pose Reaching That Works Remarkably Well

We present a sim-to-real recipe for training in-hand object pose reaching policies on a multi-fingered hand at arbitrary wrist orientations and transferring them zero-shot to the real hand. A learned residual safety filter is added to tune performance-safety trade-offs and substantially reduce object drops, with extensive real-robot evaluation.
