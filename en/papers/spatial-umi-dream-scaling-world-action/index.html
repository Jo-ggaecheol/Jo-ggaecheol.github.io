Spatial-UMI-Dream: Scaling World Action Models with Metric Geometry for UMI Manipulation

Spatial-UMI-Dream is a 5B world-action model that jointly predicts RGB, metric depth, and actions after two-stage pretraining on 605 hours of UMI data. It is evaluated on the RoboTwin-UMI simulation benchmark and real-world bimanual tasks for in-domain performance and generalization across environments and robot embodiments.

Weiduo Yuan, Ze Yuan, Sihan Liu, Jionghao Wang, Liang Heng, Zhenyu Zhao, Yue Wang, arXiv, 2025

TL;DR

  • Addresses the issue where the moving wrist camera in UMI demos entangles with object motion, and the lack of evaluation for robot embodiment and environmental changes.
  • Trains Spatial-UMI-Dream, a 5B world-action model that jointly predicts RGB, metric depth, and actions, using 605 hours of UMI data.
  • Reports a 79.10% success rate on RoboTwin-UMI in-domain, 72.15% under embodiment + environment shift, and an average of 85.0% across six real-world tasks.

Problem Definition

The Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) allows for robot-independent demo collection; however, because the camera mounted on the gripper moves near objects, camera motion and object motion become entangled. Since RGB observations only implicitly represent metric geometry, distance information becomes weak in tasks requiring precise contact, alignment, or bimanual coordination.

Furthermore, existing UMI-based policies lack controlled evaluations for changes in robot embodiment and environmental factors such as lighting. This paper proposes Spatial-UMI-Dream, which introduces explicit metric depth and combines world modeling with action prediction, and evaluates cross-embodiment and environmental generalization using the RoboTwin-UMI benchmark.

Core Idea

Spatial-UMI-Dream transforms UMI observations into a canvas where RGB and metric depth are placed side-by-side, enabling the world model to simultaneously predict future videos and actions. By explicitly separating camera motion from object motion using explicit depth, distance and alignment information necessary for precise manipulation is enhanced, making the model robust to changes in embodiment or lighting. The model generates future visual tokens and actions from a joint distribution conditioned on past visual tokens Z<iZ_{<i}Z<i​, current state sis_isi​, and text ℓ\ellℓ:

(Zi,Ai)∼pθ(Zi,Ai∣Z<i,si,ℓ)(Z_i, A_i) \sim p_\theta(Z_i, A_i \mid Z_{<i}, s_i, \ell)(Zi​,Ai​)∼pθ​(Zi​,Ai​∣Z<i​,si​,ℓ)

Methodology

Metric RGB-D Observation Construction

Each UMI gripper is equipped with three cameras. After calibrating and aligning the center and right cameras, metric depth is estimated using Fast FoundationStereo. Subsequently, it is inverse-mapped to the original center fisheye coordinate system to create depth DteD_t^eDte​ aligned with RGB.

The depth uses an encoding inspired by Marigold. After normalizing with fixed boundaries dmin⁡=0.01 md_{\min}=0.01\,\text{m}dmin​=0.01m and dmax⁡=1.5 md_{\max}=1.5\,\text{m}dmax​=1.5m, the Turbo color mapping ϕ\phiϕ is applied.

Φ(D)=ϕ(dmax⁡−clip⁡(D,dmin⁡,dmax⁡)dmax⁡−dmin⁡)\Phi(D)=\phi\left(\frac{d_{\max}-\operatorname{clip}(D,d_{\min},d_{\max})}{d_{\max}-d_{\min}}\right)Φ(D)=ϕ(dmax​−dmin​dmax​−clip(D,dmin​,dmax​)​)

The RGB and encoded depth from both grippers are arranged in a 2×22\times 22×2 canvas. Each panel is resized to 160×160160\times 160160×160, resulting in a full frame of 320×320320\times 320320×320.

Ct=[It0Φ(Dt0)It1Φ(Dt1)]C_t= \begin{bmatrix} I_t^0 & \Phi(D_t^0) \\ I_t^1 & \Phi(D_t^1) \end{bmatrix}Ct​=[It0​It1​​Φ(Dt0​)Φ(Dt1​)​]

Without a separate 3D encoder, the entire canvas is encoded into video frame tokens zt=Evid(Ct)z_t=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{vid}}(C_t)zt​=Evid​(Ct​) using the existing Wan2.2 VAE. Pixels with no valid depth are encoded as black.

Joint World-Action Model

The Wan VAE generates visual tokens, and a text encoder provides language tokens via cross-attention. A shared transformer uses block-causal attention to condition on past visual history, jointly denoising future RGB-D observations and actions.

The joint distribution introduced above is trained with a flow-matching objective. A clean target xxx and Gaussian noise ϵ\epsilonϵ are used to construct xτ=(1−τ)x+τϵx_\tau=(1-\tau)x+\tau\epsilonxτ​=(1−τ)x+τϵ, and the velocity ϵ−x\epsilon-xϵ−x is predicted.

LFM=E[w(τ)∥vθ(xτ,τ;c)−(ϵ−x)∥22]\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}} = \mathbb{E}\left[ w(\tau)\left\|v_\theta(x_\tau,\tau;c)-(\epsilon-x)\right\|_2^2 \right]LFM​=E[w(τ)∥vθ​(xτ​,τ;c)−(ϵ−x)∥22​]

The total loss is the sum of the RGB-D world prediction loss Lworld\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{world}}Lworld​ and the action loss λaLaction\lambda_a\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{action}}λa​Laction​. In joint world-action training and fine-tuning, λa=1\lambda_a=1λa​=1 is used.

Two-Stage Training

The model is initialized from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B. The entire diffusion transformer is trained, while the text encoder, image encoder, and video VAE are kept frozen.

  1. World-only adaptation
    The action branch is disabled, and robot state conditioning is removed to optimize only Lworld\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{world}}Lworld​. The goal is to adapt the backbone to moving UMI fisheye observations and the RGB-D canvas layout.

  2. Joint world-action training
    Initialized with the Stage 1 checkpoint, an action encoder/decoder and robot state conditioning are added. Subsequently, Lworld+λaLaction\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{world}}+\lambda_a\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{action}}Lworld​+λa​Laction​ is minimized.

Training is performed on 605 hours of RGB-D UMI data. Each stage consists of 30,000 steps and uses 24 NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPUs. The optimizer is AdamW with a learning rate of 10−510^{-5}10−5, using a cosine schedule with a 5% warmup at the beginning.

Demo Filtering and Deployment Compensation

Demo data is verified by replaying it in ManiSkill after inverse kinematics solving with Dexmate Vega1. Episodes violating robot kinematic limits are removed. Episodes exceeding the 99th percentile of velocity, acceleration, or jerk are also entirely deleted.

At deployment, gripper command compensation is applied. The gripper response is approximated by a first-order response model with delay δ\deltaδ and time constant τ\tauτ, which is then inverted to forward-correct the commands.

uk=clip⁡(rk+1+δ−αrk+δ1−α,0,wmax⁡)u_k= \operatorname{clip}\left( \frac{r_{k+1+\delta}-\alpha r_{k+\delta}}{1-\alpha}, 0, w_{\max} \right)uk​=clip(1−αrk+1+δ​−αrk+δ​​,0,wmax​)

Here, rkr_krk​ is the measured gripper state, α=exp⁡(−h/τ)\alpha=\exp(-h/\tau)α=exp(−h/τ), and wmax⁡w_{\max}wmax​ is the maximum opening/closing degree. With this compensation, the mean absolute tracking error on continuous target trajectories decreases from 12.49 mm to 2.85 mm.

RoboTwin-UMI Benchmark

RoboTwin-UMI maintains existing RoboTwin tasks while adjusting them for UMI observations and grippers. Two robot embodiments, Aloha-AgileX and ARX-X5, are equipped with UMI grippers and wrist fisheye cameras, and lighting can be randomized.

In simulation rendering, four coaxial pinhole views are used to create fisheye images. The field of view for the front view is 92∘92^\circ92∘, and for auxiliary views, it is 106∘106^\circ106∘. After ray tracing with the Kannala-Brandt (KB4) model, sampling is performed from the camera with the closest optical axis.

The evaluation setup uses 50 clean demos per task for Aloha-AgileX and consists of four conditions where lighting (clean/randomized) and robot (Aloha/ARX-X5) are varied independently and jointly. 100 episodes are evaluated per task.

Experimental Results

Baselines include VLA-based models such as π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​ and RDT2-FM, and WAM-based models such as Fast-WAM and LingBot-VA. All models are fine-tuned with identical demos. The action representation is a 20-dimensional relative action, including 3D coordinates, 6D rotation, and gripper opening/closing degree, with a chunk size of 24.

Real-world evaluation involves performing six tasks on Dexmate Vega1: Pick-and-Place, Bimanual Handover, Scan-and-Place, Pack-and-Close, Handover-and-Pour, and Table Wiping. 20 episodes are evaluated per task, and the progress score is the ratio of completed stages.

Critical Review

Strengths

  • Metric depth representation separating camera and object motion in UMI observations — Converts implicitly mixed camera/object motion from moving UMI fisheye RGB into explicit metric depth, injecting it into the World Action Model (Evidence: Sec 1, 2A, Fig. 3).
  • Benchmark standardizing UMI observations and shift evaluation — RoboTwin-UMI provides controllable fisheye rendering, two robot species (Aloha-AgileX/ARX-X5), combined lighting/species shifts, and per-task step limits (Evidence: Sec 2E, Fig. 6, Table IV).
  • Quantitative separation of core component contributions — Shows RGB-D improves over RGB-only by +5.75%p in-domain, +8.25%p under combined shift, and real-world from 80.8% to 85.0%, while two-stage training contributes from 39.50% to 61.25% to 79.10% (Evidence: Fig. 9, Sec 3D).
  • Data quality and gripper control signal correction — Reduces gripper tracking error from 12.49mm to 2.85mm through inverse kinematics/replay-based filtering and first-order response model compensation (Evidence: Sec 2D, Fig. 4, Fig. 5).

Weaknesses & Questions

  • Potential imbalance in baseline training costs — Spatial-UMI-Dream received 605 hours of RGB-D UMI pretraining and two-stage training, but it is unclear from the provided materials whether π0.5\pi_{0.5}π0.5​, RDT2-FM, Fast-WAM, and LingBot-VA were prepared with identical pretraining data, scale, and training steps (Evidence: Sec 3A, Table III; Appendix notes).
  • Lack of statistical significance reporting — Simulation is evaluated with 100 episodes per task and real-world with 20 episodes, but standard deviation, confidence intervals, number of repeated runs, and seed distribution are not provided in the materials, making it impossible to determine if differences like +4.65%p or +7.20%p are within noise range (Evidence: Sec 3B, 3C, Table I/II; Appendix notes).
  • Limited generalization evaluation axes — In RoboTwin-UMI, only lighting is randomized, while background, clutter, table height, and camera randomization are disabled. Shifts other than the two robot species and UMI fisheye observations are not evaluated, providing limited evidence compared to claims of “robot-independent” or broad generalization (Evidence: Sec 2E, Appendix notes on randomized-lighting).
  • Insufficient component ablation — Only metric depth and two-stage training are ablated; the effects of other design choices such as depth encoding method Φ(D)\Phi(D)Φ(D) (Eq. 1), stereo vs monocular/learned depth, robot state conditioning, λa\lambda_aλa​, and text conditioning cannot be verified from the provided materials (Evidence: Sec 3D, Fig. 9; Appendix notes).
  • Incomplete data/parameters required for reproduction — Code/data availability is not specified in the provided materials. While source of 605-hour data, stereo calibration/Fast FoundationStereo parameters, fisheye inverse mapping details, and demo filtering thresholds (99th percentile criteria) are necessary for reproduction, only some are present in the appendix (Evidence: Sec 2A, 2D; No mention of code/data release in provided materials).
  • Unreported inference/deployment costs — While training GPU count and steps are available, total training time, inference latency, policy execution speed, and test-time future imagination cost are not provided, leaving the impact on actual manipulation deployment of WAM unclear (Evidence: Sec 2C, Table III; Appendix notes).

  • Code/data availability cannot be confirmed from the provided summary.
