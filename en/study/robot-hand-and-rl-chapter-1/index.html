01Agent and Environment

Reinforcement learning can be summarized in one sentence: "Try it, get a score, and adjust your actions to increase that score." There are only two players involved.

TermMeaning🤖 In the Thumb Button Task
State sThe complete, true information of the worldAll precise positions, velocities, and contact forces of the hand, tool, and button
Observation oWhat the agent can actually seeJoint angles/velocities, fingertip pressure, motor current, button position
Action aWhat the agent choosesTarget joint changes + per-finger stiffness
Reward rA single number indicating how good the recent action wasr_reach + r_press + r_click + …
🤖 State ≠ Observation. The simulation knows the exact pose of the tool (state), but a real V6 hand only knows pressure and current (observations). Only observations should be fed into the policy, while components used only during training (reward calculation, critic, teacher) may use the state. This is what "(teacher-only) exact object pose" on the roadmap card refers to. → Q1 · Q3

Rewards are sums of multiple terms, but they ultimately result in a single scalar. Therefore, the weight of each term is important (Chapter 4: Reward Design).

03Return and Discount Rate γ

The agent's goal is not to maximize the immediate reward, but to maximize the sum of rewards received until the end of the episode. Moving the thumb toward the button may yield no immediate benefit, but it is necessary to eventually receive r_press and r_click (+10).

Gt = rt+1 + γ·rt+2 + γ²·rt+3 + ⋯ ,    0 ≤ γ < 1 Return = Discounted sum of future rewards · Rewards further in the future are discounted by γk

There are three reasons for discounting. ① The sum remains finite even if the episode is long ② The distant future is uncertain ③ For the same +10, receiving it sooner has higher value, which incentivizes ending quickly.

Practical intuition: The horizon seen by γ ≈ 1 / (1 − γ) steps. If γ = 0.99, it is about 100 steps. For the thumb button running at 50 Hz for 5 seconds (250 steps), values near 0.99 are used. Most Isaac Lab examples also use 0.99. In the Chapter 9 exercise, we confirmed that even after enduring 500 steps, the discounted return stops at 99.3.
🤖 Why rewards are given in stages. If only click +10 is given, distant rewards appear blurry due to γ, and accidental clicks are rare at first. Therefore, r_reach (approaching) and r_press (press progress) provide intermediate guidance.

04MDP and the Markov Property

MDP = ( S, A, P, R, γ ) State space · Action space · Transition probability P(s′ | s, a) · Reward function · Discount rate
SymbolNameIn the 🤖 Thumb Button task
SState spaceAll positions, velocities, and contacts of hand, tool, and button
AAction spaceJoint target changes + stiffness (continuous values)
PTransition probabilityThe physics engine serves this role
RReward functionr_reach + r_press + r_click …
γDiscount rate0.99

You don't need to know P. Even without formulas for friction and contact, you can learn by calling step() and receiving the results. This is model-free RL, and PPO falls into this category. We only need to define S, A, R, γ, and the termination conditions. Designing the problem card = defining the MDP.

Markov Property: Knowing the present is enough; the past is unnecessary

The next state is determined solely by the current state and action. However, this property is often violated when looking only at observations. Cases where observations fail to capture the full state are called POMDPs, and almost all real-world robotics problems are POMDPs.

ObservationMarkov?Reason
Joint angles only❌Cannot predict next position without velocity
Joint angles + velocityNearly ✅
+ Fingertip pressureStill insufficientPressure alone cannot fully determine tool pose
+ Previous actionBetterHelpful to know "what was just commanded" when there is motor delay

Solutions: Include velocity and previous actions in observations · Stack recent steps (frame stacking in Atari DQN, Q4) · RNNs · Teacher-student estimation of hidden physical values from history (HORA, Chapter 6).

When filling out the observation field of a problem card: "Can I predict what happens next with just this information?"

05🤖 Following a Thumb Button Episode

We follow one episode of Curriculum ① (pressing with the thumb while already holding it) to the end. The policy acts at 50 Hz, and in between, the physics engine runs 4 times at 200 Hz (decimation = 4 in Isaac Lab).

  • Rewards are not summed immediately upon receipt. Instead, (o, a, r) is stored for each step, and returns and advantages are calculated separately from each step. This allows evaluating whether "the action at t = 10 was good" independently.
  • If wandering aimlessly reaches 250 steps, it ends as truncated; if the tool is dropped, it ends as terminated (failure).

06Learning Loop: Episode ≠ Update

The policy is not adjusted at every step. Experience is accumulated for a while and then corrected all at once.

Therefore, in the early stages of learning, the thumb shakes wildly, but with repetition, the behavior settles into the sequence: "penalty decreases as it approaches → positive reward upon contact → +10 when fully pressed."

07Policy, Value Function, Advantage

Policy π: What to do in this situation

A deterministic policy always takes the same action for the same observation, while a stochastic policy samples an action from a distribution. If actions are continuous values, like a robotic hand, a normal distribution is typically used.

NeuralNet(o) → mean μ (per joint), std dev σ
a ~ Normal(μ, σ)     # During learning: shaking by σ = exploration (Chapter 8)
a = μ                # Real-world deployment: usually just the mean

V, Q, Advantage

V(s) = E[ G | s ] · Q(s, a) = E[ G | s, a first ] · A(s, a) = Q(s, a) − V(s) V = situation score · Q = after taking this specific action first and then following the policy · A = how much better this action is than usual

The comparison target for Advantage is not "now vs later" but "this action vs what is usually done here".

SituationActionQVAVerdict
Thumb 2 cm in front of buttonTowards button+7+5+2Probability ↑
Thumb 2 cm in front of buttonAway from button+1+5−4Probability ↓
Tool is tilted (crisis)Grip tighter again−1−3+2Bad, but better than usual → ↑

PPO does not have a Q neural network. It estimates using only the critic V.

At ≈ rt+1 + γ·V(st+1) − V(st) Actual result − Expectation · TD error. In practice, GAE mixing multiple steps is used (Chapter 3)

08Exploration and Exploitation

Meaning🤖 On the thumb button
Exploitation (Exploitation)The best action so far"Pressing down gave a + reward" → keep pressing down
Exploration (Exploration)Try actions not yet attemptedSideways, at different angles, varying stiffness

Relying solely on exploitation gets stuck in a suboptimal method of pressing the button's edge diagonally (local optimum), while relying only on exploration fails to utilize what has been learned.

How PPO explores

  1. σ of the stochastic policy: Decreases naturally as learning progresses
  2. Entropy bonus: "The wider the distribution, the higher the bonus" to prevent σ from decreasing too quickly. See entropy coefficient in Chapter 3.
  3. Parallel environments: 4096 instances each try different things
  4. Random start / Domain randomization: Conditions change every time, so repeating the same action does not yield a score
Learning curve signal: If entropy drops to 0 too quickly → insufficient exploration, suspect local optimum. If it remains high → still uncertain or weak reward signal.
🤖 The second role of roadmap randomization. Button position ±5 mm, actuation force ±30%, and starting pose ±15 mm are for sim-to-real transfer, but they also have an exploration effect by providing diverse experiences during learning.

CartPole

A problem where you keep a pole balanced on top of a cart by pushing the cart left and right.

Observation obs[cart position m, cart velocity m/s, pole angle rad, pole angular velocity rad/s]. Angle + = tilting to the right
Action0 push left, 1 push right (discrete. The thumb button is continuous)
Reward+1 for each step survived → return = number of steps survived
terminatedAngle > 12° or position > ±2.4
truncated500 steps (effectively a success)

Code 1: Loop (cartpole_loop.py)

import gymnasium as gym

env = gym.make("CartPole-v1")

for episode in range(3):
    obs, info = env.reset(seed=episode)   # Episode start, first observation o₀
    episode_return = 0.0
    t = 0

    while True:
        action = env.action_space.sample()   # Policy slot. Currently random
        next_obs, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)   # One loop iteration

        episode_return += reward
        obs = next_obs        # If skipped, the policy keeps seeing old observations
        t += 1

        if terminated or truncated:
            print(f"[Episode {episode}] Length {t} steps, Return {episode_return:.0f}")
            break

env.close()

What Did We Confirm?

  1. Policy = obs → action function. Once we started using obs, it survived 2 times longer than random.
  2. Why is velocity needed? Looking only at the angle means reacting after it has already tilted, causing large oscillations (40.9). Looking at angular velocity allows reacting during the fall (198.7). Looking at both achieves 500. A policy using only the angle is equivalent to partial observation where velocity cannot be seen (Chapter 4).
  3. Manually changing k is tuning. With k = 0.5 and 0.1, it achieves 500 every time. PPO automatically finds these kinds of numbers among millions (Chapter 10).
  4. The horizon of γ. Even if you survive 500 steps, the discounted return stops at 99.3. (1 − 0.99500) / (1 − 0.99) ≈ 99.3. γ = 0.99 looks roughly 100 steps ahead (Chapter 3).

10🤖 Where does the human fit in?

k is a number inside the policy, so RL finds it automatically. Reward design and shaping are indeed human tasks, but they operate on a different level than k. k answers "how to solve it," while reward answers "what counts as doing well."

Therefore, the human role shifts to the two ends: top and bottom.

  • Goal Definition: What "click" means and up to how many N it can be pressed. Derived from tool, process, and customer requirements.
  • Judgment by Observation: The reward curve went up, but watching the video shows the button is being rubbed from the side → the eye to detect reward hacking.
  • Hardware and Safety: V6 fingertip 17.3 N, heat generation during continuous operation, MCU hardware limits.
  • Real-World Verification: Does what worked in simulation work on the V6? If not, what is wrong with the simulation?
From tuning numbers to "defining what 'good' means and verifying if it's truly good."

?Check Questions

Try to recall the answer first before expanding.

Question 1If you remove the two velocities (cart velocity, angular velocity) from CartPole observations, what problem arises, and how can it be mitigated?

It becomes a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP). This is the same reason why a policy looking only at angle falls over at step 41. By stacking recent observations (frame stacking), velocity can be estimated from the differences.

Question 2If γ = 0.9 and the policy runs at 50 Hz, what is the horizon in seconds? How much is a +10 click reward after 55 steps worth now?

1/(1−0.9) = 10 steps = 0.2 seconds. 10 × 0.955 ≈ 0.03. Since the click is barely visible, this is too short-sighted for a thumb button. With 0.99, it would be 10 × 0.9955 ≈ 5.8. (Check Figure 3)

Question 3What are the terms for ending due to dropping the tool versus ending because 250 steps have passed? Why distinguish them?

Terminated and truncated. After dropping, there is no more reward to receive, but time-out implies "reward that would have been received if continued." Mixing them causes the critic to incorrectly learn that "step 250 has an inherent value of 0." Detailed handling is in Chapter 3 GAE.

Question 4In front of the button, an action has Q = +3, and the situation's V = +5. Even though the reward is positive, does the probability of this action increase?

No, it decreases. A = 3 − 5 = −2. It is a worse-than-average action. The criterion is not "is it positive?" but "is it better than average?"

Question 5r_push (angle the tool rotated in hand) cannot be measured on the actual V6. Can it be used in reward? In policy observations?

It can be used in reward. Reward is only used during learning, and simulation knows the ground truth. It must not be put in policy observations because that value won't exist in the real world. If you want to include it, put it only in teacher observations and let the student estimate it from pressure/current history.

Question 6Between k in the rule-based policy and −1.0/cm in r_reach, which one does PPO find?

k. k is a number inside the policy. −1.0/cm is a problem-side value determining "what counts as doing well," so it is set by humans (or searched via external loops like Eureka).

Q&AQuestions & Answers

Questions I asked myself while studying. Try to recall the answer first, then expand.

Q1Why do we distinguish between the true state of the world and observations? Does it affect learning? Can't we just use observations?01 · Agent and Environment
  • It is correct to put only observations into the policy. In real-world deployment, you have nothing but observations.
  • Reason for distinguishing ① If observations cannot fully capture the state, learning fails (POMDP). Without velocity, you don't know if you are approaching or moving away. → Figure 4
  • ② Rewards are only used during training, so they can be calculated using the state. r_push cannot be measured in real-world deployment, but the simulator knows it.
  • ③ Critics and teachers are only used during training, so they can accept the state. The roadmap's "(teacher only)". → Figure 7
Q2If actions are output continuously in small increments, does a new observation and reward occur at every step, which we then accumulate and use to correct the policy? Please summarize one cycle from start to finish with an example02 · Episode
  • Yes. The policy outputs actions at 50 Hz, and a new observation and reward occur at every step. → Figure 5
  • However, rewards are not summed immediately; instead, we store (o, a, r) and then calculate step-wise returns and advantages.
  • The policy is not corrected at every step. We collect data from 4096 environments × 24 steps with the policy fixed, then correct it all at once. Episode ≠ Update. → Figure 6
Q3For reward calculation and things like the critic that don't go to real-world deployment, do we evaluate by finding the true score using state values?02 · Episode
  • Yes. The method of giving the state to the critic is called asymmetric actor-critic.
  • Note ① Rewards are not "true scores" but "scores we define". If the formula is wrong, the agent will become good at doing the wrong thing (reward hacking, Chapter 4).
  • Note ② Real-world success judgment must be created using observations. Roadmap principle: "Simulator success judgment should use the same signals as the real-world judge (pressure patterns, click force changes, current)."
Q4Is it like Atari problems where we accumulate a bit of past information to estimate the state?04 · MDP
  • Yes. DQN (2015) for Atari couldn't know the ball's velocity from a single frame, so it stacked the most recent 4 frames and input them (frame stacking).
  • Robots can directly input joint velocities, so shorter history is often sufficient.
  • Hidden physical values like friction and mass are solved with long history + learned estimators (HORA's adaptation module, Chapter 6).
Q5V is the situation score, Q is the average score when acting according to this policy in this situation? Is Advantage evaluated based on what, and is it state-based?07 · Policy and Value Function
  • V is correct. Q is not "according to the policy" but "take this one action first, then follow the policy afterwards".
  • Advantage is not "now vs later" but "this action vs usual actions here". Even an action that results in less damage in a crisis situation has A > 0.
  • The evaluation target is not the entire policy but the single action at that step.
  • PPO calculates A ≈ r + γV(s′) − V(s) using critic V. If we input state into V, advantage also becomes based on correct information. → Chapter 7

Q7Explain the code line by line. When actually designing RL, where do I need to write and tune directly?09 · Practice
  • Key lines: reset() = episode start, action = … = policy slot, step() = one loop iteration, obs = next_obs = if missed, you only see old observations.
  • This code has no tuning spots. Values determined by the designer (observations, actions, rewards, termination, time limit, start distribution, physics) are inside the environment. → Chapter 9 Table
  • Learning hyperparameters (γ, lr, rollout length, entropy) are added here (Chapters 2 & 3).
Q8What does obs mean, and what is Task 1 asking us to do?09 · Practice
  • obs = observation. [cart position, cart velocity, pole angle, pole angular velocity], angle + = tilting to the right.
  • Task 1: Create a rule-based policy that decides actions by looking at obs instead of randomly. If the broom tilts to the right, move the hand to the right.
  • Purpose: To feel that policy = obs → action function, and to see that RL is about letting a neural network find this if-statement.
Q9Is branching actions based on observation values creating a simple "Markov branch"?09 · Practice
  • Acting only based on current observations → Markov policy (memoryless). Same obs means same action → deterministic policy. Switching abruptly when a threshold is crossed → bang-bang control in control theory.
  • Results: Random 17/17/37 → Angle rule 41/51/35. Looking only at angle, it reacts after tilting, so it doesn't reach 500.
Q10In the AI era, is the human role in RL just setting values like k? Do reward design and shaping fall under this?10 · Human Role
  • k is found by RL. It is a number inside the policy.
  • Reward design and shaping are indeed human tasks, but they are at different levels. k = "how to solve it", reward = "what to count as doing well". → Figure 11
  • Writing and iteratively modifying reward code is something LLMs do well (Eureka). Humans move towards goal definition, spotting reward hacking by watching videos, hardware/safety, and real-world verification.

✓Confusing Concepts

  • Observation ≠ State: Policy uses only observations, rewards/critic/teacher can use state. Q1 · Q3
  • Rewards are stored then calculated: Store (o, a, r) at each step → calculate step-wise returns/advantages. Q2
  • Episode ≠ Update: Rollout is with fixed policy, update uses thousands of episode fragments. Q2
  • Markov is a property of observations: One angle photo vs (angle, angular velocity). If insufficient, use frame stacking. Q4
  • Q is "this action first", Advantage is "compared to usual". Even with positive reward, A < 0 is possible. Q5
  • Designer-defined spots = Environment: Observations/actions/rewards/termination/start distribution/physics. Q7
  • Rule-based policy: Markov + deterministic policy, bang-bang control. Q9
  • k vs Reward weight: Policy parameter (found by RL) vs Problem setting value (set by human). Q10
